ANTI-AI DESIGN SYSTEM — HUMAN-DIRECTED CREATIVE STANDARD

PRIMARY OBJECTIVE

Create websites that look deliberately designed by an experienced human designer, not generated by an AI website builder.

The finished website must feel individual, distinctive, commercially credible and appropriate to the specific business.

A visitor should not immediately recognise familiar AI-generated design patterns.

Do not simply produce a technically competent website with attractive colours. Produce a website with a recognisable creative direction, intentional visual decisions and its own identity.

The objective is not to make the website look unusual for the sake of it. The objective is to make it look intentional rather than predictable.

---

1. BAN GENERIC AI WEBSITE PATTERNS

Avoid automatically using the following unless there is a compelling, business-specific design reason:

* Oversized centred hero headings with generic supporting text.
* Purple, blue or pink gradient backgrounds.
* Glowing or blurred background orbs.
* Excessive glassmorphism.
* Floating dashboard mockups.
* Random floating decorative elements.
* Repetitive rounded cards containing icons and short descriptions.
* Three-column feature grids repeated throughout the page.
* Identical rounded buttons everywhere.
* Excessive pill-shaped elements.
* Huge empty spaces without a design purpose.
* Generic stock photography.
* Random abstract geometric decorations.
* Unnecessary borders around every section.
* Excessive use of shadows.
* Identical section layouts repeated down the page.
* Generic AI marketing phrases such as "Elevate your business", "Unlock your potential" and "Transform your digital presence".

Do not replace these patterns with different clichés. Make decisions based on the business.

---

2. CREATE A DISTINCT CREATIVE DIRECTION

Before writing components, establish a design direction based on:

* The industry and target audience.
* The personality of the business.
* Its commercial positioning.
* The emotional response the website should create.
* The type of customer it needs to attract.
* The actual products or services being sold.

Choose a coherent visual identity.

Consider:

* Editorial design.
* Contemporary Swiss-inspired layouts.
* Industrial and architectural design.
* Premium minimalism.
* Bold typographic compositions.
* Asymmetrical layouts.
* Magazine-inspired compositions.
* Brutalist-inspired design where appropriate.
* Photography-led layouts.
* Custom graphical elements.
* Distinctive typography.

These are creative references, not templates. Do not blindly copy any particular style.

The visual direction must make sense for the business.

---

3. TYPOGRAPHY MUST HAVE CHARACTER

Avoid automatically using the same typography hierarchy on every website.

Rules:

* Establish a deliberate typographic system.
* Use the H1 as the primary visual headline.
* Use eyebrow text only when it adds context.
* Do not make subtitles larger than the main headline without a clear creative reason.
* Avoid making every heading enormous.
* Use different font weights and sizes intentionally.
* Consider editorial typography, distinctive font pairings and varied line lengths.
* Avoid excessive letter spacing.
* Do not make every heading bold.

Typography should feel art-directed, not generated from a standard component library.

---

4. LAYOUT VARIATION IS MANDATORY

Do not use the same section structure for every client.

Actively consider:

* Asymmetrical compositions.
* Full-width image sections.
* Split-screen layouts.
* Overlapping content.
* Horizontal visual storytelling.
* Offset image and text arrangements.
* Editorial image grids.
* Strong typographic sections.
* Alternating light and dark sections.
* Full-bleed photography.
* Carefully controlled whitespace.
* Unusual but intuitive content positioning.

Different sections should have different visual rhythms.

Do not force every piece of content into a card.

Do not create a three-column grid simply because there are three features.

Do not make every section follow the same left-text/right-image structure.

---

5. MAKE COMPONENTS FEEL PURPOSE-BUILT

Components should feel like they belong to this particular website.

Avoid creating a generic collection of reusable SaaS components.

For example:

* A fitness business should feel energetic, physical and motivational.
* A construction company should feel established, dependable and practical.
* A premium service business should feel refined and confident.
* A creative business should feel expressive and visually distinctive.
* A professional consultancy should feel authoritative and trustworthy.

Build visual elements around the actual business, not around what is easiest to generate.

---

6. PHOTOGRAPHY AND IMAGERY

Images must have a purpose.

* Prioritise genuine client photography and supplied assets.
* Use relevant, high-quality imagery.
* Consider image cropping, composition and aspect ratios.
* Avoid generic stock photos of people smiling at laptops.
* Avoid decorative images that add no information.
* Use image treatments consistently.
* Do not force images into identical rounded rectangles.
* Avoid using AI-generated imagery as a substitute for genuine business photography where authenticity matters.

Where appropriate, use custom graphics, diagrams, textures or visual storytelling instead of generic stock images.

Never invent client credentials, premises, staff or products.

---

7. COLOUR SHOULD BE INTENTIONAL

Do not automatically default to the familiar AI palette of purple, blue, pink and white.

Build the palette around the business.

Consider:

* Brand identity.
* Industry conventions.
* Customer expectations.
* Contrast and readability.
* Background variation.
* Accent colour restraint.

Use colour to create hierarchy and atmosphere.

Do not use gradients simply to make a design look more sophisticated.

---

8. MICRO-INTERACTIONS AND MOTION

Use motion selectively.

Good examples:

* Subtle entrance transitions.
* Purposeful hover states.
* Refined image reveals.
* Interactive navigation.
* Meaningful scroll-based storytelling.
* Carefully considered button feedback.

Avoid:

* Animating everything.
* Constant floating animations.
* Excessive parallax.
* Unnecessary scroll-jacking.
* Decorative motion that slows the experience.

Performance, accessibility and usability always take priority.

---

9. COPY MUST SOUND HUMAN

Do not produce generic AI marketing copy.

Avoid:

* Repetitive sentence structures.
* Unnecessary adjectives.
* Empty claims.
* Overuse of em dashes.
* Repeated three-part phrases.
* Generic claims about excellence and innovation.
* Repeating the same benefit in multiple sections.

Write for the actual customer.

Use specific, understandable language. Explain what the business does, why it matters and what the customer should do next.

Never invent statistics, testimonials, qualifications or guarantees.

---

10. DESIGN VARIETY WITHOUT SACRIFICING CONVERSION

This is not an exercise in artistic experimentation.

Every design decision must support:

* Immediate understanding.
* Customer confidence.
* Clear positioning.
* Easy navigation.
* Trust.
* Conversion.

Do not sacrifice usability to achieve visual originality.

The website must still have a clear customer journey and obvious calls to action.

---

11. THE AI PATTERN DETECTION TEST

Before considering the design complete, critically inspect the website.

Ask yourself:

1. Could this website easily be mistaken for another AI-generated website?
2. Does it use predictable AI landing-page patterns?
3. Are the sections visually repetitive?
4. Does the typography have a deliberate identity?
5. Are the images relevant and authentic?
6. Does the layout reflect the actual business?
7. Does every visual element have a reason to exist?
8. Does it look like somebody made creative decisions rather than accepted the first generated design?
9. Would a professional web designer be comfortable presenting this to a paying client?
10. Does it feel distinctive without being difficult to use?

If the website fails, redesign the weakest areas before finishing.

Do not simply add more animations, gradients or decorative elements to make it look different.

---

12. FINAL CREATIVE DIRECTIVE

Do not optimise for what AI websites usually look like.

Optimise for what the best human-directed website for THIS particular business should look like.

Every client should receive a recognisably individual website.

Reuse good engineering, accessibility standards, responsive systems and proven conversion principles.

Do not reuse the same visual identity, section compositions or creative direction across clients.

Build like a designer. Think like a marketer. Execute like an experienced frontend engineer.

The finished result should feel bespoke, commercially effective and deliberately art-directed.

HOW THIS APPLIES IN THIS REPOSITORY

* The platform rules in `CLAUDE.md` win over this skill: costs need approval (no paid fonts, stock libraries or image services), dates are DD/MM/YYYY and times 24-hour UK, no legal claims the platform cannot back, nothing is deployed without the owner's approval.
* A business's website is built from its own content (`lib/siteContent.js`) and design direction (`content.design.direction`, drawn by `lib/siteBuilder.js`). Never ship one fixed look for every business. Add or refine directions instead of tuning one.
* Business photos come only from that business's own `brand-assets` folder. Never invent credentials, reviews, vehicles, staff or premises: draw only what the business entered.
* Fonts are self-hosted and free (the repository's Jost files, and system font stacks). Do not add a hosted font link.
* Keep the booking form usable, accessible (contrast, focus, reduced motion) and above the fold on phones: conversion comes first.
